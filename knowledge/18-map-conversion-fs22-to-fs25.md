# 18. Map conversion FS22 → FS25 (roads, terrain, traffic, trees)

Learned while converting an FS22 base map to FS25 for private use.
Status markers: ✅ confirmed in game or in the GIANTS Editor, 📖 read from the FS25 base map files, ❓ assumption or not yet confirmed in game.

## Roads

### Roads as terrain decals
- ✅ A road shape without collision becomes a terrain decal with `terrainDecal="true" renderedInViewports="false" castsShadows="false"` and no `static` or collision attributes. Every vertex is projected onto the terrain.
- ✅ `renderedInViewports="false"` is required. Without it the road floats in game and vehicles drive through it.
- ✅ Overlay pieces such as junction patches use `decalLayer="1"`.
- ✅ The terrain under a decal road must match the original road surface exactly, or the road becomes bumpy. Read the FS22 road meshes from the `.i3d.shapes`, rasterise the triangles onto the 1 m DEM, and set the terrain to road surface − 0.01 m. Add a 3 m blend ring around the road.
- ❌ Raised details do not work as decals. Traffic islands with curbs and separate road-marking geometry get flattened and look wrong. Keep such roads as 3D meshes with collision.

### 3D roads with collision
- 📖 The FS25 EU map uses 3D meshes with collision for its main roads (`mainRoad_*`, `intersection*`). Only side roads and junction patches are terrain decals.
- 📖 Base game road collision is `collisionFilterGroup="0x601c" collisionFilterMask="0xfffffbff"`, bridges use `0x207c`. FS22 roads often use `0x3e`.
- ✅ With `0x3e`, FS25 traffic cars ignore the road mesh and sink to the terrain. This is most visible on ramps and at decal↔mesh transitions where the road sits above the terrain. Switching the road to `0x601c` fixed it. Both base values contain bit `0x2000`, which the traffic probably uses (❓).
- ✅ Terrain can poke through a 3D road between the 1 m grid points even when it matches at every grid point. The fix:
  - Evaluate the terrain the way the engine renders it (two triangles per cell, use the maximum of both diagonals if unsure) and compare it with the road surface every 10 cm.
  - Lower only the grid vertices that actually poke through, to 2 cm below the road.
  - Let the lowering fade out by 6 cm per metre outside the road.
- ✅ Lowering all terrain under a 3D road by a fixed amount creates visible edges at the road border.
- ✅ **Final recipe for terrain under 3D roads and at decal↔mesh seams** (confirmed perfect in game):
  1. Fit the terrain to road surface − 0.01 m under decal and 3D roads.
  2. Lower only the inner part of each 3D road by 12 cm. Leave the border and the seams alone.
  3. Apply the poke-through fix above.
  4. **Fit the seams by least squares:**
     - Seam points are boundary edges of the 3D road within 0.25 m of a decal road boundary edge, sampled every 0.2 m.
     - Unknowns are all grid vertices within 3 m of a seam point.
     - Seam equation: terrain (bilinear) at each seam point = edge height + 2 mm, weight 10.
     - Mesh equation: every third mesh surface sample 0.3–1.6 m behind the seam, terrain = surface − 8 mm, weight 3.
     - Add closeness to the start state (weight 0.05) and smoothness between neighbours (weight 0.5). Solve with `scipy.sparse.linalg.lsqr`.
  5. Run the poke-through fix again (2 mm extra, 5 mm clearance), skipping the 0.3 m strip right behind the seam. There the terrain may rise up to 9 mm above the road.
  - Computed result: average seam gap 0.5 cm, at most 3.3 cm (before: up to 26 cm). More rounds bring no further gain.
  - Traffic is not affected by the lowering once the road uses collision group `0x601c`.
  - Intermediate states tested in game: steps 1–3 alone left gaps and steps at the seams. With step 4 at edge − 4 mm, most seams looked clean and a few showed a very thin gap.
- ❌ Variants that looked worse in game:
  - Removing the lowering and running the poke-through fix on the unlowered terrain: steps at the seams.
  - Terrain at exact mesh height at the seam: shows through as lighter patches.
  - Blending only the decal side: a 1–2 cm step.

### Snow on decal roads ❓ not yet tested in game
- 📖 Vertex colour B is snow. FS25 base roads: B = 1 at the edges (|t| ≥ 2.8 m) and on the centre line, B = 0 in each lane (|t| ≈ 1.4 m), linear in between → wheel tracks in snow. FS22 roads only have edge 1 / centre 0.
- To convert, split FS22 road strips along |t| = 1.4 and 2.8 m (lateral position from the texture v coordinate, which runs linearly across the 8 m asphalt) and recompute B; handle T-crossing decals from their local geometry.

### Asphalt and verge vegetation
- ✅ Paint an asphalt terrain layer under all roads, otherwise tyres leave dirt tracks on the road. A per-pixel coverage threshold of 0.02 worked. Higher thresholds made the painted area too wide, lower thresholds left stripes.
- ✅ Remove grass on the road but keep the original verge vegetation up to the curb. Do not paint over field paths or bushes. A GIANTS Editor Lua script with `DensityMapModifier` and `DensityMapFilter` can copy the states from the FS22 density map.
- ✅ In the FS22 road meshes, vertex colour B is a wear blend (0 = centre, 1 = edge), not opacity.

### Road material (`vertexPaintShader`)
- 📖 Vertex colour R = opacity of `mBlendedDiffuse`, G = softness, B = snow, A = noise gradient (`alphaNoise` variations).
- FS22 used the `terrainFormat` variation without blended maps. In FS25, set `mBlendedDiffuse` and `mBlendedNormal`.
- ✅ Check the road vertex colours first. In the converted FS22 roads, R was 0 everywhere, so `mBlendedDiffuse` never shows.
  - The dirt at field entrances came from separate `dirtTracks` decal shapes (`decalShader`, `alphaBlending`), which look paler in FS25.
  - ✅ To make them follow the terrain like the roads, set them to terrain decals as well and put them on `decalLayer="2"`, above the road decals (layer 1).
  - ❓ To darken them, use `<CustomParameter name="colorTint" value="-0.07 -0.075 -0.055"/>`; in the FS25 `decalShader`, `colorTint` computes `saturate(diffuse + XYZ)`.

## Traffic
- 📖 FS22 traffic splines can be reused as they are. In the converted map they sat on the road surface within a few centimetres.
- The traffic root node needs the user attribute `xmlFile` pointing to `trafficSystem.xml`. The EU map also sets `onCreate = TrafficSystem.onCreate`.
- ✅ FS22 AI licence plate files are structured differently from FS25 ones (FS22: `licensePlateXX/…Front` transform group with `diffuse_decal` + `body`; FS25: `licensePlateXX/licensePlateFront` shape with child `diffuse_decal`). Using the FS22 file stopped all traffic; an FS25 AI plate file fixed it. FS22 player plates work.
- 📖 Density reference: the EU map uses `maxNumVehicles="15"` on 15.3 km of traffic splines, about 1 car per km. Asia (54.8 km) and US do not set the attribute. The schema (`trafficSystem.xsd`) allows at most 30.
- 📖 Traffic vehicles: `dumpTruck` and `cementTruck` in `$data/trafficVehicles` are US-style conventional (bonneted) trucks, `tipperTruck` is a European cab-over truck. The EU map's `trafficSystem.xml` still lists all three; leave out `dumpTruck` and `cementTruck` for a purely European look.
- Spline length can be measured from the `.i3d.shapes`: entity type 2 is a spline (name, align4, shapeId, form, count, then `count × float3` control points in local space).

## Trees
- ✅ FS22 trees use the old `collisionMask="16791586"`, which FS25 ignores, so the trees have no collision. With the base game values `collisionFilterGroup="0x830" collisionFilterMask="0xfe3ffb83"` they stop vehicles and can be felled (confirmed in game).
- The oak split type (7) is the same in FS22 and FS25. Other FS22 split type numbers were not checked.

## Forestry and rock contracts (new in FS25)
- 📖 FS25 maps list `deadwoodMission`, `treeTransportMission` and `destructibleRockMission` under `<missions>` in the map XML. Each one points to a spots i3d.
- Map templates often ship these commented out, with spots that belong to another map. Make your own spots for a converted map.
- Spots are empty transform groups:
  - Deadwood: the centre of a forest.
  - Tree transport: the origin sits at the edge of a flat, clear area next to a road, with `rotation="0 ry 0"`. Children `tree1…n` use `rotation="-90 -90 0"`, so they lie along local x. User attributes are `numRows`, `numTreesPerRow` and `sizeX`/`sizeY`/`sizeZ`. Point local z away from the road so the logs lie parallel to it.
  - Rocks: a clear, flat meadow.
- ❓ Spots can be found automatically:
  - Deadwood: tree density from all tree reference nodes, 200 m minimum spacing, excluding field polygons (`gameplay/fields`) and water.
  - Transport: 9–13 m from a road, height span < 0.8 m over 15 m, no tree < 9 m, no object < 18 m.
  - Rocks: 25–120 m from a road, flat.
  - ✅ Deadwood and tree transport contracts on such self-made spots work in game.
- ✅ Deadwood spots need a float user attribute `radius` (base game uses 75–95). Without it, the log warns "No radius defined for deadwood mission spot".
- 📖 Destructible map rocks (`onCreate = DestructibleMapObjectSystem.onCreateGroup`, `groupId`, `dropFillTypeName = STONE`) sit visibly on the terrain in the base maps. Check that template copies are not buried under the new terrain.

## Collectibles, pedestrians, licence plates
- 📖 FS25 collectibles (EU map golden apples):
  - Each collectible is a trigger shape (`static`, `trigger`, group `0x20000000`, mask `0x100000`, `nonRenderable`) with user attributes `name` and `onCreate = Collectible.onCreate`, plus a visual child.
  - The target is a group with `onCreate = CollectibleTarget.onCreate`, holding one child per collectible name. That child becomes visible once the collectible is found.
  - `collectibles.xml` links each `name` to its `target`.
  - FS22 used a visible shape with `triggerIndex` instead.
- 📖 The pedestrian system format did not change between FS22 and FS25: a `pedestrianSystem` group (`onCreate = PedestrianSystem.onCreate`, `xmlFile`) with splines (`density`, `groupName`, `width`). FS22 splines can be reused; take `pedestrianSystem.xml` from an FS25 base map.
- ✅ The `blender-i3d-importer` shapes reader returns triangle indices starting at 1. Subtract 1 when writing an inline `IndexedTriangleSet`, otherwise GIANTS Editor 10 crashes silently on import (the log ends with "non-binary indexed triangle sets").
- ✅ Inline geometry in an import i3d (`IndexedTriangleSet`, `NurbsCurve` with `<cv c=…/>`) imports fine in GIANTS Editor 10.
- ❓ FS22 licence plates:
  - Point the material shader to `$data/fs22support/shaders/vehicleShader.xml`. FS25 ships this FS22 vehicle shader, including the `colorMask` and `Decal` variations.
  - Copy the FS22 textures into the mod.
  - FS25 has the fonts `LICENSE_PLATE_FR/DE/PL/CZ/UK/US/AS`.
  - FS25 plate XMLs use `<material materialSlotName>` instead of FS22's `<color shaderParameter>`; whether the old element still works is unconfirmed.

- ✅ When you copy and rename an FS25 i3d, also update `<Shapes externalShapesFile=…>`, or every shape is missing ("Failed to open shapes file").
- ❌ `$data/fs22support/shaders/vehicleShader.xml` failed to compile for FS22 licence plates (variations `colorMask`/`Decal`: "cannot convert from float4 to float3"). Build the plates from an FS25 plate file instead (font `LICENSE_PLATE_FR` etc.).

## Map placeables
- ✅ Register the mod's own placeables in the modDesc under `<storeItems>` with mod-relative paths. In the map `placeables.xml`, reference them as `$moddir$ModName/...`. The map `storeItems.xml` only resolves `$data` paths.
- ✅ Farm buildings get a `uniqueId` in `placeables.xml`, as in the base maps.
- ✅ A material with `staticLight` caused "Graphics Pipeline State" errors. A plain material with `emissiveColor` works.

## Practical notes
- The GIANTS Editor crashed importing a whole FS22 map. Import only the needed group (e.g. the traffic system) saved as its own i3d.
- Raycasts in the GIANTS Editor do not hit temporarily static shapes, so editor scripts cannot fit terrain to roads by raycasting.
- After changing the DEM, delete the `*.terrain.*.cache` files next to the map i3d.

## Start fields of the player farm
- FS22 had no file for this: the crops were painted into the map's `densityMap_fruits.gdm`. FS25 sets start fields in `maps/config/fields.xml` (`<field fieldId>` with `<fruit type growthState>`, `<ground type angle>`, `<spray>`). State names are the `foliageState` names in capitals (`HARVESTREADY`, `HARVESTED`). 📖
- Read the FS22 state: convert `.gdm`/`.grle` to PNG with `grleconvert` (github.com/Paint-a-Farm/grleconvert). FS22 fruit pixel value = R + 256·G; fruit type = value & 31 (order of the `FoliageType` entries in the FoliageMultiLayer with `numTypeIndexChannels`), growth state = value >> 5 (FS22 wheat: 8 harvest ready, 9 withered, 10 cut). Ground type = value & 15 (2 cultivated, 11 harvest ready). Sample inside each FS25 field polygon (`polygonPoints`).
- ✅ The `fieldId` in `fields.xml` is the **farmland id**, not the field number (mapEU: farmlands 45/46 with `defaultFarmProperty` ↔ fieldId 45/46). With field numbers the start fields stayed empty.
- ✅ "pedestrian can't find the next spline to walk on": FS22 pedestrian loops whose start and end points coincide are stored as open splines (`form_closed` = 0). With no other spline near the end, FS25 pedestrians get stuck there. Closing these splines (flag bit 0) fixed it: no more errors in a 26-minute session.
- The `.i3d.shapes` encryption (I3DCipher) is an XOR key stream per 64-byte block: changing one plaintext byte changes only the same ciphertext byte. A spline entity (type 2) is name (int32 length + bytes, aligned to 4), shapeId, uint32 flags (bit 0 = closed), point count, points. So a spline in a huge map `.shapes` can be closed with a one-byte patch instead of rewriting the file. Decrypt entity by entity with the same chunking as the writer (count, then per entity type, size, data) to find the file offset.

## Enlarging a map to 4x (2 km -> 4 km) ❓ not yet tested in game
- The world origin is the map centre, so existing objects keep their coordinates; only terrain files grow. 📖
- Differences between the GIANTS template `maps` and `maps_4x` (📖 compared directly): DEM 2049 -> 4097 (unitsPerPixel stays 1);
  every density/info map doubles its edge (fruits/ground/height etc. 4096 -> 8192, farmlands/soilMap 1024 -> 2048,
  navigation/placement collision 2048 -> 4096, environment 512 -> 1024); weight maps 2048 -> 4096;
  `lodTextureSize` 4096; sun `depthMapResolution` 4096 and `lastShadowMapSplitBboxMin/Max` ±2048;
  `DisplacementLayer cellSize` 2 -> 4; border walls at ±2000; map XML `width/height` 4096.
- The template border walls are planes ±1024 m long centred on their origin, so `scale="2 1 2"` at ±2000 covers 4 km. 📖
- Padding: decode .gdm/.grle to PNG (grleconvert), put the old map in the centre, re-encode with `--i3d`. The grleconvert round trip is lossless by its own decoder, but the encoded file still needs the two fixes below before FS25 reads it correctly.
- Rename `$moddir$<modName>/` paths in vehicles.xml / placeables.xml when the copy gets a new mod name.
- Terrain continuation: a Laplace fill on a coarse grid plus noise with a ~1/f² height spectrum looks natural; flatter spectra look like grain.
- ✅ grleconvert pitfall: when encoding a `.gdm` it writes header byte 13 (number of type-index channels) as 0. For foliage multi-layers with `numTypeIndexChannels` (fruits 7, groundFoliage 5) FS25 then refuses the file ("GDM file has wrong number of channels after type index channel conversion (14 + 7 - 0 should be 14)") and every foliage type is missing. Set byte 13 to the layer's `numTypeIndexChannels` after encoding. GDM header: [8] log2(size)−5, [9] log2(chunk), [10] max bpp, [11] channels, [12] compression ranges, [13] type-index channels.
- ✅ grleconvert pitfall 2: chunks (32×32) with more than 4 distinct values are written as raw values with only as many bits as the largest value needs (3, 4 …). GIANTS files only use bit depth 0/1/2 with a palette, or the **full width of the compression range** (fruits 7+7, groundFoliage 5+5). FS25 misreads the narrow chunks: in mixed areas (decorative grass, field margins) strips without foliage appear, and the in-game map shows coloured crop pixels in horizontal bars there. Fix: rewrite every palette-less chunk to the full range width (lossless; verify that all pixels decode equal). Chunk layout: [bit depth][palette count][palette u16…][bitmap, bit depth·128 bytes, LSB first].
- ❓ Merging fields (not yet confirmed in game): a field is a node under `gameplay/fields` with `polygonPoints` (points relative to the field node, y = terrain height − node y), `nameIndicator` and `teleportIndicator`; its UserAttributes `polygonIndex`/`nameIndicatorIndex`/`teleportIndicatorIndex` are child indices. Keep one node per group, replace its points with the outline of the union including the strips between the fields (buffer out and back in with mitred joins), delete the other nodes and their UserAttributes. The in-game field number is the farmland id, so `infoLayer_farmlands`, `farmlands.xml` and `fields.xml` (`fieldId`) must be updated together. To change a few areas of a `.gdm` without re-encoding the whole file, rewrite only the affected 32×32 chunks with GIANTS-style encoding (palette for ≤ 4 values, otherwise full range width).
- ❓ The loading screen of a mod map is a crop of the map's `preview.dds` (`<map><iconFilename>`): full width, only roughly the bottom 41 % is visible above the info bar (estimated from one 2560×1440 screenshot). Put any marker (e.g. a "4x" badge) into that band.
- ❓ Start vehicles from a savegame: `maps/config/vehicles.xml` uses the same schema as the savegame's `vehicles.xml` (`savegame_vehicles.xsd`). Copy farm-1 vehicles with `defaultFarmProperty="true"`, keeping `component`, `configuration`, `licensePlates`, `fillUnit` and, for attached implements, `uniqueId` plus `attacherJoints/attachedImplement`; drop wear/dirt, other mods' data and configurations added by mods. Start field fertilisation: `<spray type="FERTILIZER" level="2"/>` (savegame `sprayLevel` runs 0–2).
- ✅ `overview.dds` covers **twice the map width**: the playable area is the central half of the image, the rest shows the surroundings beyond the border. For a 4x map (4096 m) a 4096 px overview therefore means 2 m/px over 8192 m. An overview that only covers the playable area appears doubled in size in game (field overlay only in the centre).

## Extending a river and a railway beyond the old map edge ❓ not yet tested in game
- FS22 maps often contain **background water planes** that run far beyond the map edge (where the background panorama used to be). They show the course the map author intended – a good guide for continuing the river after enlarging the map. They are not always flat (heights varied for the background effect), so replace them with a flat plane at water level. Swapping one shape inside a large map `.i3d.shapes` can be done by re-serialising just that entity and copying all others unchanged.
- The **train spline is a closed loop**; its return section usually runs outside the map. After enlarging the map, that section ends up inside the terrain (the train disappears into the meadow at the old edge). Route the return section behind the new map edge again, underground or outside the terrain. Track meshes and catenary meshes typically also run a little past the old edge and bend into the hidden return – they have to be cut back.
- Track cross-section of the FS22 French map (`railroadStraight50mGravel`): ballast bed 5.54 m wide, rails at ±0.71–0.89 m, rail top 0.126 m above the centreline; U repeats every ~24.3 m. Sweep this profile along a new centreline for extensions.
- Carving a river valley into a heightmap: copy the cross-section of the existing river (flat bed, cosine bank up to the water line, floodplain, valley side) and blend into the old terrain with a smooth minimum. Do **not** use hard `clip` limits on noise fields that drive width or slope – the kinks show up as arc-shaped ridges along the noise contours; use `tanh` instead. Clear decorative foliage below the water line, and give the water plane a generous margin when the shoreline is noisy.
- ❓ Extending a railway: cut the existing track mesh back at the last catenary pole before it bends into the hidden return (drop triangles whose centroid lies beyond the cut along the track direction), then sweep the cross-section of a straight template piece (two rings of vertices) along the new centreline, carrying all vertex channels (normals and tangents rotated into each frame, U continued with arc length and offset to match the last ring of the old mesh). Take the start height from the old mesh ring (rail head minus its local height), not from the train spline, which can sit a few centimetres higher. Catenary poles in the FS22 French map have their origin on the track centre with local +Z pointing against the spline direction, spaced ~40 m; the contact wire hangs ~5.9 m above the rail centre with ~0.5 m sag and ±0.28 m stagger.
- ❓ The train spline must stay a closed loop: route the return section outside the terrain and well below the edge height. Build it from a polyline with Chaikin-rounded corners – a cubic spline through a few far-apart points overshoots badly (also vertically).
- Decorating new riverbanks like the original: measure the existing river first (tree density per metre of bank, species mix, distance from the water line, rock size/sinking/cluster spacing, foliage states per distance band) and sample the same distributions along the new banks, using coherent noise so trees, rocks, reeds and flowers appear in groups rather than evenly scattered.
- **Statistics are not enough – transfer real sections instead.** Painting weights and foliage from distance tables gives the right averages but looks smooth and washed out ("clear-cut" look). Original GIANTS maps have metre-scale structure: terrain weights are dithered per pixel (mostly 0/255), rock streaks follow the bank edge, the river bed is rough, micro-relief of 2–10 m, dense rock rows at the water line, tree clusters with gaps. Better: split the new centreline into blocks (~50 m); for each block and each bank/track side pick a validated anchor on the original river/track (water-line point with normal towards land, or track centre) and map target (u, t) to anchor + u·tangent + t·normal. Transfer the height *deviation* from the original mean cross-section (added to the carved profile so the height still fits the surroundings), weights per pixel with nearest sampling (never bilinear, and pick one source per pixel between overlapping blocks instead of averaging – averaging blurs the dithering), decorative foliage types only, and tree/rock nodes with rotated orientation. Cross-fade blocks over ~20 m and mirror the original across the old edge for the first ~20 m so the seam is invisible.
- Validate anchors strictly: no roads/yards (other weight layers), no fields (use the field polygons from the i3d – many fields start empty in `densityMap_ground` and otherwise show up as brown rectangles on the bank), no props; for rivers check water/land on the expected sides. Railways in farmland usually have a field or road on one side, so validate and transfer each track side separately. Fold deep river-bed samples back towards the bank so they never reach the opposite shore of the template.
- Git Bash on Windows rewrites arguments starting with `/` into Windows paths (`/scene/node` → `C:/Program Files/Git/scene/node`); set `MSYS_NO_PATHCONV=1` or run through `cmd /c`.
- Do not carve a railway as a gorge with fixed embankment slopes – it looks forced into the landscape. Original GIANTS lines keep the terrain level with the sleepers for many metres to both sides (median flat out to 30 m). Keep the track close to the terrain, keep a flat shoulder next to the ballast, then blend into the old terrain with a smoothstep whose width grows with the height difference (e.g. 6 m + |Δh| / 0.16, i.e. no steeper than ~1:6). Where the terrain climbs faster than the allowed grade this produces a wide valley instead of a cutting. Limit transferred template relief to small amplitudes (soft clamp ~±1.5 m) so larger features of the template do not end in a visible edge.
- Avoid lines parallel to a new railway: template relief taken from an existing line contains ditches and ballast edges that run parallel to the track and turn into grooves at a fixed offset; a constant shoulder width or a constant-width smoothstep also leaves parallel creases. Do not transfer template relief along railways, vary shoulder and blend width with low-frequency noise along the line, use a quintic smootherstep (continuous curvature), and fade the trackside textures out very gradually (dithered over 50–60 m). Check from eye level – top-down hillshades hardly show such shallow grooves.
- When trees and rocks are placed from different template blocks they can end up on top of each other. Afterwards remove every tree whose trunk lies inside a rock footprint (rock local extent × scale, plus a small margin), checking existing rocks too, and verify in the final i3d.
- Blending the old map edge into an enlarged area: FS22 maps usually have a border wall inside the edge (here 50–80 m wide, up to 40 m high, falling towards the edge) and a coarser heightmap at the rim. Plain mirroring creates a crease exactly on the edge (the slope flips) and a second wall outside; odd mirroring digs a trench. Keep the existing outer terrain instead, remove the step at the edge with an exponentially decaying offset, continue the slope of the last couple of metres inside for a short distance (smoothed along the edge), and mirror only fine detail relative to the edge point (zero at the edge, clamped). Verify by measuring the slope jump across the edge all the way round.
- For direction-less land, transfer template patches on a 2D grid (random rotation per cell, smoothstep weights with a plateau so neighbours sum to one) instead of a strip mapping along a curve – strips repeat once you fold beyond the validated window and fan out at corners. Exclude orchards (tree rows) from templates. Where carved valleys meet the edge, also blend their side slopes at the edge, keeping the water channel and the track bed untouched.
- The outermost rows of an FS22 heightmap often deviate by 1–3 m from the surrounding slope (a thin wall or trench exactly on the edge – it even covered a railway track here). After enlarging, replace a narrow strip across the old edge (about 6 m inside to 5 m outside) row by row with a cubic Hermite curve that matches height and slope at both ends, and lift/lower objects standing in it.
- Foliage and terrain weights also have a uniform outermost band (here ~2 m of a single decoFoliage state); mirrored outward it shows as a line in the grass. Replace a band of about ±5 m around the old edge row by row with content from ~10 m further in/out (skip roads and fields).
- After mirroring or copying foliage near tracks, clear the track again (ground foliage within ~4.5 m, crop/grass foliage under the ballast) – mirrored bushes from beside the track otherwise end up on the rails. Large bare patches copied from template field margins can be filled with the value of the nearest vegetated pixel. Do not use the old train loop's hidden return section as a track reference after it has been replaced.

## New roads onto an enlarged map area ❓ not yet tested in game
- First check whether any road actually ends at the old edge: in many FS22 maps the traffic network is a closed loop inside the border wall (no spline end without a neighbour within 3 m). New roads are then branches; branching from existing T-crossings in their free direction keeps the network tidy.
- Find crossings via the crossing decals (`roadTCrossingStop`/`GiveWay`); arms = clusters of traffic-spline points on a 22–32 m ring around the centre, free direction = largest angular gap. Spline ends alone are blurred by the two lanes.
- Typical FS22 decal road cross-section (`roadsVP_mat`): 7 vertices per ring at ±4.0 (v 0.484/0.984), ±3.7 (v 0.503/0.966), 0 (v 0.734) plus a separate centre-line strip ±0.075 m raised 2 cm (v 0.034/0.044); vertex colour (0,1,B,1) with B = 1 at the edges; U along the road, 1 unit per 16.27 m, tangent = direction of increasing U. v 0.484 is on the right in driving direction.
- Turning a T-crossing decal into a 4-way crossing: copy the stem triangles (all corners local z ≤ −3.99) rotated 180° about Y (x, z → −x, −z) – do not mirror, otherwise the stop line ends up on the wrong lane. The main road strip already has identical edge strips on both sides.
- Vertical alignment: low-pass the terrain along the route (σ ≈ 35 m), then clamp the grade **sequentially** forward and backward (a vectorised clamp only propagates one sample per pass), round with a small Gaussian and clamp again; ramp the allowed grade up over ~30 m when leaving the crossing. Cut/fill with a flat carriageway plus a varying shoulder and a quintic transition whose width depends on the height difference at the shoulder edge (not per pixel) and varies with noise per side.
- Copy terrain weights and foliage beside the road pixel by pixel from straight interior road sections (reject windows with other roads, crossings, field polygons, water or paving/concrete layers); put the asphalt layer under the decal footprint and clear foliage there.
- If the map has no AI road splines, AI helpers use the navigation map; add traffic splines only once the new road connects somewhere (dead ends are risky for NPC traffic).
- FS22 maps place trees in two ways: as ReferenceNodes and as TransformGroups with LOD0/LOD1 shapes. When you modify terrain, adjust both, otherwise the second kind floats.
- Rasterise the asphalt weight under a new decal by pixel coverage (supersample, ≥ 50 %), not with a filled polygon plus dilation – that made the asphalt about 1–1.5 m too wide per side.
- A build pipeline must not read inputs from the mod folder it writes to; otherwise each run feeds back into the next (here: template masks picked up the new asphalt and the edge blending changed). Read everything from a fixed backup and compare checksums of intermediate results.
- Level crossing for a new road: place a copy of the existing crossing floor (long axis along the track, top = rail head) on the track centre, keep the road flat at rail-head height for ±8 m, leave the terrain beside the ballast untouched, and put the crossing signs on the right of each approach.
- Check the terrain gradient before routing: where the ground rises faster than the allowed road grade, a straight road turns into a canyon; follow valleys or contour lines instead.
- GRLE info layers (8 bit): 21-byte header ('GRLE', u16 version 1, u16 width/256, u16 0, u16 height/256, u8 0, u8 1, 3 zero bytes, u32 data length); data = value a, and if the next byte repeats a it is a run of 2 + sum of the following bytes (255 = continue). The SDK grleConverter only decodes, so a small encoder is needed to edit farmland maps.
- Splitting a field with a road: clip the polygon against the road line (half-plane clipping with a gap), keep the old field node and its attributes for one part, add a new field node with a copy of the UserAttribute block for the other, renumber that side in the farmland info layer and add the farmland to farmlands.xml (field number = farmland id).
- Height pins (crossing, level crossing) close together must allow at least the required grade between them, otherwise the limiter leaves a step at the pin.
- Level-crossing gates live in the train system placeable (a `railroadCrossingNN` group in trainSystem.i3d plus a `<railroadCrossing rootNode=…>` entry with gates, blockers, signals and sounds in trainSystem.xml). Append a copied group as the last child so the indices of callers and hotspots stay valid.
- Where the ground rises faster than the road may, flatten broadly (slope width from height difference over a small mean slope, e.g. 10 %, up to a few hundred metres) instead of cutting a canyon; check slopes numerically, exaggerated cross-sections are misleading.
- Paint rock layers pixel by pixel on steep new cut slopes (probability rising from ~30°), clear foliage there and place a few large rocks on the steepest spots.
- FS22 level-crossing floors look poor under the FS25 renderer even with identical data. Replacing them with the FS25 base-map wooden crossing planks works well: bake the asset's Rx(−90) into the vertices, place the plank deck flush with the rail head along the track, and keep the old floor slightly lower as an asphalt strip next to it. Take the crossing list from the train system (gate groups sit on the track centre at rail-head height).
- After transferring templates or painting rock layers, clear crop/grass foliage wherever rock weights dominate, otherwise grass hides the rock texture.
- Where a road runs above a field, leave the field at its height and turn the short strip between them into a rocky edge; between two halves of a split field, blend the edges broadly and gently instead.
- Level crossings: remove the FS22 floor slab entirely (it creates bumps and edges), flatten the terrain along the road at rail-head height, paint asphalt and clear foliage there, then put the plank deck on top. FS22 gate bases sit at ±4 m along the track; for an 8 m road move them out to about ±5.8 m.
- When a cut crosses a hill whose ground keeps rising sideways, grow the slope width until the ground at every distance is reached with the target slope, otherwise a wall appears — but not where a natural slope runs parallel to the road.
- After merging FS22 fields, the old boundary often survives as a thin meadow strip and grass texture inside the new polygon; clear decorative grass and grass weights inside field polygons.
- Nearest-centreline terrain shaping creates a crease along the bisector of tight corners; blur the terrain change outside the carriageway and smooth width changes along the road.
- Traffic markers are dynamic objects with breakable joints; after terrain edits move them (and all signs) with the ground or they topple on load.
- At level crossings only raise the terrain inside the ballast band under the deck itself, otherwise ballast and sleepers next to the deck disappear.
- i3d Euler rotations compose as Rz·Ry·Rx (verified on an FS25 base-map crossing plank). With yaw θ and pitch α, local z maps to (sinθ·cosα, −sinα, cosθ·cosα), so α = −atan(gradient).
- Crossing gate groups are not exactly at rail-head height (±10 cm) and tracks can be inclined at crossings; fit rail-head height and gradient from the rail vertices and place/tilt the deck 1 cm below the rail head so the rails stay visible.
- Some FS22 traffic markers sit a few centimetres inside the terrain; in FS25 their breakable joints snap on load. Lift all sign objects to at least 2 cm above the ground.
- When transferring verge templates beside a new road, keep existing dirt-track pixels beyond the shoulder so field tracks still reach the road.
- The FS25 plank crossing (railroadCrossingWood02) has wood up to ±1.27 m and checker-plate strips beyond; keep only the wood triangles if a pure plank look is wanted and let asphalt meet the planks.
- To make field tracks reach a new road, fill the verge gap by searching outward perpendicular to the road for the first track pixel and copying its ground, then clear foliage there.
- The FS25 plank crossing has a checker-plate base plate spanning its full size (tiled UVs); filter it by texture coordinates, not by triangle position, and centre the deck on the measured track centre so the rail-to-edge distance is equal on both sides.
- Dirt-weight based track detection also picks up field soil; exclude field polygons from it.
- Check which texture a terrain layer actually uses before painting it where it will be visible: in a converted FS22 map `asphalt01` pointed to the US asphalt texture, which looks like checker plate in FS25; `tarmac01` (EU) is the better visible asphalt.
- Dirt next to a road is usually a verge, not a field track; treat only dirt that is narrow along the road direction as a track. Clear ground foliage (bushes) inside field polygons after transferring verges.
- FS22 road decals can contain their own level-crossing triangles mapped to a checker-plate region of the road atlas; they replace the asphalt there. Remap their UVs to the asphalt region when using plank decks. Prove the cause with data (render the object's texture, read terrain layers at the spot, list nearby triangles with UVs) before changing things.
- FS22 dirt areas are dithered pixel by pixel; detect tracks on a blurred dirt density (≥ 50 %, low density beside it, continuous away from the road) instead of single pixels.

### Making terrain build scripts use all cores (bit-identical output)
- Profile first (`python -m cProfile -o x.prof script.py`). The typical hotspot is a helper that builds `cKDTree(points)` on every call. Build it once, cache it, and query with `workers=-1`. In one case that took a step from 82 s to 32 s.
- Load and save weight PNGs through a `ThreadPoolExecutor`. PIL decoding and zlib compression release the GIL, so threads are enough.
- Decrypting `.i3d.shapes` is single-threaded, roughly 10 s per large file. Cache the parsed meshes, keyed by path, size and mtime, when the source files never change.
- Keep random-number streams on one thread so the output stays reproducible. Verify with md5 against the old run.

### Level-crossing planks and track beds
- Set the plank height from the rail-head vertices only within the plank length (about ±7.5 m), then lift the plane to the highest head underneath. A wider fit picks up neighbouring track pieces at other heights. Straight FS22 track pieces have vertices only every ~20 m, so interpolate between the nearest vertex on each side. Take the plank top from the mesh itself; leave about 2 mm below the rail head so vehicles do not bump.
- When new roads copy roadside strips from template areas, reset weights and foliage within ~3 m of the track centreline to their previous state afterwards, except under the planks and asphalt. Otherwise grass ends up growing between the rails.
- Paint crossing asphalt and clear foliage only on paved pixels and under the planks, not in a rectangle. FS22 roads often have a gravel verge stored as ground foliage.
- Remove road-decal triangles under the crossing planks. Split them at the plank edges and interpolate every per-vertex attribute, including `uv1` on FS22 shapes. Otherwise asphalt shows through the flangeways between planks and rails. Cut the dashed centre line only where its u coordinate falls in a gap of the dash pattern, so no dash ends half-cut at the planks. Lower the terrain under the planks and paint it as gravel so the gaps look dark.
- Align planks with the actual rails, not with the shape bounds. The FS25 `railroadCrossingWood02` mesh is ±2.01 m wide, but its top surface spans only about x −1.36…+1.32. Its flangeways are off-centre and slightly skewed (about 0.17°, 3.5 cm over 11 m), so measure the groove centre per position along the plank from the top-surface triangles. Find the track centre per rail from the rail-head vertices (top 1.5 cm of each vertex row) and fit a minimax line, for example with `scipy.optimize.linprog`. On curves a straight plank cannot follow the track, so build one plank shape per crossing: split it every 0.5 m along its length and move the vertices sideways onto the track centre and vertically onto the rail heads (gradient, crests, cant). Then the node only needs a yaw rotation, which also avoids getting the sign of a pitch rotation wrong. End road decals 1–2 cm under the measured plank edge and a few millimetres below its top, so road and planks meet flush.
- A shape with `terrainDecal="true"` is drawn onto the terrain in game, not at its own vertex heights, so a terrain dip shows up as a dip in the road. Where the 1 m heightmap cannot satisfy both sides of a hard edge (flangeways 0.8 m from the track centre need low terrain, the plank edge 1.33 m out needs flush terrain), cut the last metres of the road decal into a separate non-decal shape. Use the same road material (bridges already do this), make it static with collision, set its heights yourself and subdivide it along the track. Then solve the surrounding heightmap nodes as a small linear programme: bilinear weights per sample point, hard upper limits in the flangeways, behind the planks and under planks and road pieces, and an objective that stays close to the smoothed surface. Cap it a little above the smoothed surface to avoid bumps, with a lower bound against absurd depths. Adding a flangeway floor to the plank mesh (about 6 cm below the top) relaxes the terrain limit there considerably. When grouping rail vertices into rows, split on gaps rather than fixed bins, or a row can fall across a bin edge and a rail-foot vertex is read as the rail head.
- How the base game does it (FS25 mapEU, sawmill crossings with `railroadCrossingWood02/03`): there is no road decal at the crossing, only painted terrain. The terrain is flat about 9–10 cm below the plank top under and beside the planks, including the flangeways, and drops to 25–35 cm in the track bed at the plank ends. A uniform step looks cleaner than trying to make a 1 m heightmap flush with the planks. In `railroadCrossingWood02` the base plate (tiled UVs, top 3.5 cm below the planks) is the floor of the flangeways. Keep it, but trim only the plate (never the planks) just inside the outer plank edges, which are asymmetric (about −1.36 / +1.32): cutting everything at ±1.36 left a sliver of plate and opened the outer plank. Also remove the plate's vertical end faces with wood UVs at |x| 1.35–2.01 m, which otherwise stand next to the planks. Do not add a separate flangeway floor: the plank ends are staggered, so a continuous floor sticks out at the ends. Keep the terrain at least 2 cm below the plate. To compare with your own crossings, sample the base-game `dem.png` (heightScale 255, 1 m per pixel, centre 1024) in the plank's local frame.

### Extending roads to the map edge ❓ not yet tested in game
- Extend an existing decal road by computing old and new part as one road (append centreline points): height, U and cross-section continue without a seam.
- Clamp every work window at the edge: the DEM has 4097 samples (up to +2048 on a 4 km map), weight/farmland maps only 4096 (up to +2047).
- Letting the road mesh run ~15 m past the terrain edge (straight along the end direction, vertices at edge height) avoids a visible road end at the border.
- Make per-road parameters (minimum embankment width etc.) piecewise along s with a soft blend, so exceptions of the old part do not apply to the whole extension.

### T-junctions onto new decal roads ❓ not yet tested in game
- Copy the map's T-crossing decal (main road along local x ±9 m, 8 m wide, branch towards −z up to 9 m) per junction and bend it along the curved main road: vertex (x, z) → centreline(s_j + σx) + z·ẑ(s) with ẑ = x̂ × up (a proper rotation; mirroring puts the stop line on the wrong lane). Leave a gap s_j ± 9 m in the main road mesh so the edges meet exactly.
- Branch mesh starts 9 m from the junction centre; pin its height to the main road for the first ~13 m and do not touch terrain within 4 m of the centre. Connector roads do the same at their end.
- Swept track meshes that are only generated when writing the .shapes file do not exist yet for later build steps; recompute the sweep there if a level crossing needs the rail heads.
- When restoring track-bed vegetation after road building, only use train-spline points that actually have rail geometry nearby — invisible splines otherwise restore grass across a road.

### Bridges in new roads ❓ not yet tested in game
- Base-game bridge files can be placed as a ReferenceNode to the $data i3d (as the US map does, e.g. mapUS bridgeRiver.i3d: deck at local y 0, 116 m along local x incl. abutments). Old map bridges may be body + separate 3D road piece rotated 90°.
- Pull the road exactly onto the bridge axis over the deck length, end the decal at the deck ends and pin the height to the deck (enough clearance; raise it when the far bank is steep).
- Do not touch terrain under the deck; at the ends use narrow, steep fills and never fill the river bed beside them — a wide embankment fan cut at a straight line leaves earth wedges in the water.
- When planning, check the grade between fixed heights (level crossing, junction): slope-only path costs can produce links that need 20 %.

### Building a long viaduct from base-game pieces ❓ not yet tested in game
- When a link cannot reach a fixed junction height at a sane grade, bridge the whole valley: write an own i3d in the mod that reuses the header/materials of mapUS `bridgeConcreteLong.i3d` (copy its .shapes next to it) and places N deck pieces (one 15.94 m piece shape), two abutment pieces and one pier per joint, piers scaled in y down to the terrain. Reference it once from the map.
- Keep the deck straight and level (straight pieces on a curve leave wedge gaps); disable level-crossing detection under the deck.

### Bridge ends, junction stubs and level crossings (lessons from testing)
- For bridges with abutment blocks, fill terrain only up to the deck end (hard edge) so the abutments stay visible; slide the road decal ~0.6 m under the bridge deck and flatten the road crown near the bridge to avoid a dark seam. The mapUS bridgeRiver deck is bare concrete: add an own 3D road strip on it.
- Branch roads must leave the T-decal stub straight for the first ~18 m, and the stub itself should stay rigid (not bent along a curved main road), otherwise a grass gap or kink appears.
- At level crossings, replace old checker-plate decal sections by a proper road strip (kerbs, centre line) between the decal ends and cut it under the planks.

### Collision data when adding shapes, crossing markings (lessons from testing)
- A new shape created from a template shape by copying its entity also copies the template's attachment block, i.e. its cooked collision. A static collision node then collides with the template's hull (here: plank decks got the hull of an 8.6 m power pole). Write an own convex-hull `cm` block for collision shapes and an empty attachment (4 zero bytes) for visual-only shapes.
- Static collision on shapes without cooked data logs "Mesh cooking …" at load. Prefer simple unscaled box colliders with a cooked hull (nonRenderable) next to the visual parts.
- Static collision needs a precooked triangle mesh (`tm` attachment). With an empty or convex (`cm`) attachment the engine logs "Mesh cooking" and the shape has **no** collision. A convex hull (`cm`) is only used with `kinematic="true"` (the base maps' collision boxes do this). For custom colliders build convex pieces (boxes, cross-section prisms) with a `cm` hull, `kinematic="true"`, `nonRenderable="true"`. Shapes that only lost triangles keep a valid `tm`.
- Rocks on slopes: derive the rock's principal axes from its LOD0 mesh; align the longest axis with the contour line, tilt the shortest axis towards the terrain normal (~65 %), and lower it until the bottom third is below the terrain. Copied/mirrored rocks keep rotations that no longer fit the new slope.
- Many base-map selling points are trigger-only placeables (warehouseEU, groceryStore, sellingStationRestaurant, sellingStationTrain); the building is a ReferenceNode in the map i3d (e.g. `mapEU/textures/buildings/grainSilo/grainSilo.i3d` for the warehouse, `smallGroceryStore.i3d` for the grocery store). Place building and trigger together.
- `warehouseEU` is not a selling point: it only has a `palletBuyingStation` (buy boards, cement, fabric …, hotspot `SHOP`). `warehouseAS` (`placeables/mapAS/warehouseAS`) both sells products (`sellingStation` with SELLINGSTATION_PRODUCTS, tipper and pallet trigger) and sells pallets; its building in the Asia map sits at the same transform: `maps/mapAS/textures/buildings/palletLoadingWarehouse/palletLoadingWarehouse.i3d` plus `...Props.i3d` (40 × 25 m, trigger on local −z). Good for a goods yard. Add it to the map `storeItems.xml`.
- Placeables taken from other base maps (mapAS/mapUS) show a black hotspot image because their `<image>` is `store_empty.png`. Base maps set the picture in `placeables.xml` as a child element: `<placeable ...><customImage filename="data/placeables/mapAS/sugarMillAS/mapIcon_sugarMillAS.png"/></placeable>` (no `$`, `.png` extension although the file is `.dds`). Icons are `mapIcon_*.dds` in the placeable folder or under `maps/<map>/textures/buildings/<building>/`.
- After enlarging a map, move the train `drivingRange startNode/endNode` (and the train selling hotspots `worldPosition`, wood/pallet train triggers) along the rail spline to the new map edge; the base EU map ends the range about 60 m inside the edge.
- Rail grain silo as in the base EU map: `railroadStorageSilo01` plus building `mapEU/textures/buildings/grainSilo/grainSilo.i3d` at silo-local (9, −12.9) with the same rotation; the track centre lies at silo-local z = −24.75 along local x, the truck side is local +z. Take the track centre from the train spline, not from the rail bed mesh.
- Pallet loading hall at the rail as in the Asia map (warehouseAS + palletLoadingWarehouse): track centre at hall-local z = +14.5 (back wall at 12.9, so the dock is 1.6 m from the track centre), hall 0.11 m below the train spline. EU silo heights: silo +0.16 m, building −0.04 m relative to the spline. On a graded track give each building its own flat yard level at rail height.
- Catenary at rail-side buildings: the EU map keeps the poles outside the building ends and runs the wire through the silo portal; the Asia map has no catenary. Where a dock is close to the track, turn the poles to the other side of the track.
- A green "plinth" along FS22 rails comes from grass bands in the FS22 rail texture used on the rail-bed slopes; in FS25 terrain it looks wrong. Replace the bands in diffuse/normal/specular with ballast from the same texture instead of repainting the terrain.
- When terrain edits keep a fixed protection band around roads/rails (e.g. 5.5 m / 3.5 m), junctions and rail-side yards keep a strip of old terrain: a ridge where a track meets the road, a gap beside the ballast. At junctions protect only the asphalt (~3.4 m) or ballast bed (~2.3 m), ramp the track to road height over the last ~25 m, and extend yards to the ballast toe.
- Decorating new sites like the base maps: the US and Asia maps dress productions with ReferenceNodes to prop files (`$data/maps/*/textures/props`, `textures/shared/props`, trees). Find the original site (a `preplaced_<name>` group, a reference to the placeable i3d, or the map placeables.xml; the EU map uses map variants without "Placeable" in the name, e.g. `dairy.xml`), convert nearby props into the building's local frame and keep their height above ground via the original DEM. Take only props standing on the ground or attached to the same building. The EU map mostly embeds its props as inline shapes, which cannot be referenced. FS25 has no parked cars as props.
- Parked cars: the traffic system parks cars on `TransformGroup name="parkedCar"` points inside `trafficSystem/parkedCars` (car length along local z), using vehicles with `probabilityParked="1"` in `trafficSystem.xml`. Add one point per parking space.
- Embedded EU-map decoration can be imported like the editor's export/import: copy the node subtree from mapEU.i3d, copy each shape raw from mapEU.i3d.shapes into your own .shapes file under a new id, renumber the materials and reference the textures/shaders by $data paths. Skip parts whose materials come from /placeables/ (they duplicate the production model) and roads/splines/traffic.
- Fences like the editor fence tool: FS25 fences are `newFence` placeables (pole `1>0`, 2 m panels `2>0`/`2>1` along local z). Base maps embed finished fences as individual panels and poles. Import the panel/pole subtrees from `fenceXX.i3d` (raw shape import), place a vertical pole every 2 m and tilt each panel around local x to follow the terrain; stretch the last panel via scale z. EU industrial fence: fence11 (welded mesh on concrete), concrete wall: fence18. Caution: fence materials with `customShaderVariation="vertexOffset..."` expect shader values set by the fence tool per panel; embedded copies then lose the mesh (only plinth and poles show). Use the same textures without vertexOffset (fence11: material 4 -> 3, 5 -> 2).
- Avoid intersecting props by placing everything through one occupancy raster (footprint from the geometry); reserve buildings, triggers and truck lanes first, and build yards completely flat.
- Props spaced evenly along an edge look artificial; base maps use small dense clusters (barrel heaps, pallet stacks of varying height, containers at building corners) with gaps and slight rotation. Do not drop fence panels for paths or slopes (leaves holes with lone poles) and use one panel variant per fence. Driveways should curve from the yard gate and meet the road at a right angle (road direction from the principal axis of nearby road points), with flared corners and side slopes.
- 3D check without the game: export the map i3d/.shapes, placeables and a terrain patch (dem + ground layer colours) of an area as a vertex-coloured PLY and render oblique and eye-level views headless with Blender (`blender -b -P script.py`). Do not filter `visual_lod` groups as LODs (they contain LOD0). Transferred props must be placed on your own terrain height, not the original DEM offset (original yards often had raised ground meshes). Step fence panels vertically instead of tilting them.
- Base-map decoration density differs: US productions carry their own yard deco (40–110 props: light poles around, pallets at the doors, barrels/crates at walls), EU productions only 7–23 (they look busy because of the surrounding village), Asia productions sit in town (only take ground props). For EU-style yards copy whole finished prop groups of the EU map (the `props` groups of debrisCrusher, carpenter, grainElevator, dairy, spinnery, farm shed corners) and set them with their long side against the yard edge.
- ❓ Copying decoration from base maps: GIANTS props almost always lean against something (wall, fence, road) that is missing at the new site. Keep a prop only inside the yard and within ~3.5 m of a real building wall (footprint from triangle edges up to 2.5 m above the base, not the bounding box) or the yard edge, and test every part of a group on its own. Many EU-map `props` groups are wall fittings (fluorescent/industrial lights 2.5–6 m up, cameras, small AC units, lightning grounding): use only groups with at least 6 ground parts. Set each part to its own height above the source terrain (group roots often sit on a different terrain level, otherwise crates sink 0.6 m); parts above 0.4 m only if another kept part lies below (stacks). Never split a group whose children are LOD0/LOD1 (all levels would show). For placeables without test/level areas, leave `tipOcclusionUpdateArea` out of the fallback footprint (oilPlantUS: 39 m too large), and make the yard rectangle always enclose the building area, or paving is left outside the fence.
- ❓ Vineyards need vine placeables: GRAPE and OLIVE are vine fruits and only show on `$data/placeables/orchards/grape/grapeSingleton.xml` (type vine, a fence specialization with fixed 3 m panels). No base map preplaces vines; preplace them with the PlaceableFence savegame schema `<fence><segments><segment start="x z" end="x z" first="true" last="true"/>`, several rows as segments of one placeable, with a GRAPE field below for the growth state. A road curb that is part of the decal texture runs across driveways; cover it with a short strip of the same material using the asphalt v coordinate.
- Yard driveways on sloped or curved roads: never use one road height. Take the height of the nearest road point for every terrain cell, run the slope normalised from the road edge (0) to the yard edge (1), and exclude this strip from any later blend back to the original terrain near roads.
- ❓ EU-map production decoration is not referenced props but embedded parts in `/map/locations/<building>/<building>Props` (waste containers, bench, break table, wall boxes and floodlights, constructionMaterial01–17, containers, decals). For the same placeable, set every part in the same pose relative to the building; keep wall parts only where the placeable geometry is tall enough there, put ground parts on your own terrain, and leave out GIANTS' curbs and parking lines. Placeables standing on a concrete slab (largest flat area 0.3–1 m up) need a terrain ramp up to the slab.
- Buildings beside a graded track: tilt them with the track (rotation "0 yaw grade") so long docks and portals run parallel to the rails.
- Vineyards: contours of the real terrain are too noisy for roads and parcels. Use a heavily smoothed terrain (σ ~24 m) for the zone, pick one smoothed guide contour and lay all vineyard roads as parallel offsets at a fixed spacing (~70 m); parcels are the strips between two roads (~140 m wide, straight sides, grass strips, rows downhill via the field `angle`). Check for overlaps at inner bends with an occupancy raster.
- Train selling is virtual: `trainSystem.xml` `<drivingRange sellingStationId=…>` sells bulk cargo when the train reaches the end of its driving range; logs and pallets on wagons are sold by triggers along the track (`woodTrigger trainSystemId=…`, `palletTrigger autoUnload="true"`). A goods yard next to the track is a loading place, not a selling point.
- Preplaced placeables (map placeables.xml) do not level or paint the terrain. Level the site yourself (flat yard, limited embankments), pave it and clear foliage/trees. Take the footprint from the placeable's test/level areas and the access side from its unload/pallet triggers, and turn that side towards the road.
- To keep sites from looking cut into the landscape: flatten only the building footprint (+ a few metres), let the rest of the yard follow the terrain gently (≤ 5 %), round the corners, use 1:3 embankments, blur the height change outside the flat core (σ ≈ 3 m), scatter the ground transition (gravel → dirt/grass) and keep grass on the embankments.
- Wider is better: an S-shaped transition over 25–90 m (scaled with the height difference) and σ ≈ 6 m blur looks natural. Fade the change to zero towards protected road/rail bodies (otherwise a step appears at the shoulder), keep the yard close to road height, and lock finished yards so a neighbour's transition does not move the ground under an already placed building (floating plinths/walls).
- Placeables from other base maps (e.g. oilPlantUS, sugarMillAS, grapeProcessingPlantAS, sellingStationRestaurant) are not in the global store; a map using them must list them in its own `storeItems.xml` (`$data/…` path), otherwise the log says "not defined in store items" and they are not loaded. A pallet-only selling station must not list bulk fill types (e.g. GRAPE) or the log warns "does not have a regular UnloadTrigger".
- Adding a field: node under `gameplay/fields` with `polygonPoints` (relative to the field node), `nameIndicator` (Note) and `teleportIndicator`, plus a UserAttribute copied from an existing field (angle, missionAllowed, missionOnlyGrass, child indices). The field number is the farmland id: paint it into `infoLayer_farmlands`, add `<farmland id>` to farmlands.xml and the start state to fields.xml (e.g. `<fruit type="GRAPE" growthState="GREENBIG"/>`). When simplifying a closed outline with Douglas-Peucker, split the ring at its farthest point first.
- In crossing decals, place raised markings 2 cm above the road surface beneath them (not above the terrain at their own vertices) and split the decal densely across the cambered road, otherwise the road pokes through stop lines.
- Later build steps must use the rail geometry after earlier trimming steps; trimmed-away track still counted as track in our case.

### How FS25 joins roads and bridges
- 📖 In the FS25 EU map (shortBridge, creekBridge01) the road is one continuous static 3D road mesh that runs from the land over the bridge and onto the land again; the bridge model has no road surface of its own, so there is no decal-to-bridge seam.
- Reproduce it by switching the road from decal to a 3D mesh some 20–25 m before and after the bridge, with the same rings, UVs and profile; on land keep it a few mm above the terrain (flush at the decal end, ramping to ~2 cm), on the bridge at deck height.
- To cut an existing road decal cleanly, split its triangles along a straight cutting line first, then drop the triangles on one side.

### Decal road networks and bridges: where to switch to 3D
- Switching from a terrain-decal road to a 3D road mesh in the middle of a road shows a visible edge (different shading), and a 3D road on land needs terrain that never pokes through between its vertices. FS25 main roads avoid this by being 3D everywhere. With decal networks, put the switch exactly at the bridge end (a natural joint): decal up to the deck edge (pin the last metre to deck height, since bilinear terrain sampling would read the lowered pixels under the bridge), 3D road only on the deck, same rings, no overlap.
- In a 3D road mesh, a raised centre-line strip shows its texture in the dash gaps as dark stripes; keep only the dash parts.
- Under the whole bridge width keep terrain below the deck (also after smoothing), fill the abutment ends fully and clear foliage.

### Bridge edge: the terrain sets the decal height
- Terrain decals are drawn on the terrain; their vertex heights are ignored. At a bridge end, make the terrain itself flat at deck height for several metres before the edge (no road crown) and keep it at deck height under the first metres of the bridge before lowering it. Lowering right at the edge creates a dip from bilinear sampling (visible seam/step).
- mapUS `bridgeRiver`: the deck spans only ±45 m; 45–57.5 m are abutments without road surface that expect earth fill up to deck height.

### Overlap, do not butt-join, decal and 3D road
- An exactly flush butt joint between a terrain-decal road and a 3D road mesh still shows a thin seam and a gap in the centre dash. Let the decal continue ~0.4 m under the 3D road and keep the 3D road ~1 cm above it there; 1 cm is enough to avoid z-fighting and the lip is not noticeable.

## Planning a road network for new map area
- Agree on a sketch first, main roads only; farms and field tracks come later.
- Use a least-cost path (Dijkstra on a 4 m cost grid: squared slope, water expensive, rail expensive plus a 30 m buffer) only
  for the rough corridor. **Do not smooth each segment between waypoints separately** – it leaves a sharp corner at every
  waypoint and at every junction. Fit one cubic B-spline per road through support points every ~120 m, raise the smoothing
  until a minimum radius (150 m) holds, add straight lead-in points so the road continues tangentially from an existing road
  or meets the road it joins at a right angle, and snap junctions exactly onto the computed centreline.
- Dead-end spurs to the map edge do not read as a network, and a tight ring around the old map looks like a bypass. More
  believable: radial continuations plus cross-links 600–900 m further out that pass through the corners, with real junctions.

## Natural scenery along new roads, GIANTS style ❓ not yet tested in game
- The FS25 base maps ship complete in the game folder (`data/maps/mapEU/data`: dem, every `*_weight.png` at 1 m, fruits density map at
  0.5 m, groundFoliage/stones/ground). Real GIANTS FS25 forests can therefore be transferred pixel-exactly. A converted FS22 map has all
  mapEU terrain layers (forestMossGround, forestRoots, forestMossGroundLeaves …), so weights map 1:1 by layer name.
- mapEU pattern: forest on hills, slopes and hollows, open farmland between, roads along forest edges or through them. Forests hold only
  ~40–60 trees/ha (large beech/oak/aspen, stages 4–6); the ground is ~75 % grass01/02 with moss/root/leaf patches, foliage mainly
  forestPlants and decoBush. An FS22 map like Haut-Beyleron has little forest but tree rows along about half of its roads
  (~11 m spacing, 4–8 m from the asphalt edge).
- Converting foliage values: mapEU fruits uses 6 type-index bits (state = value >> 6), a converted map often 7 (state = value >> 7) with a
  different type order – remap by FoliageType name. States are the 1-based `<foliageState>` entries of the foliage XML; when the XMLs
  differ (map-local meadow/grass vs. meadowEU), remap states by name. mapEU decoBush sits in fruits; on maps with a separate
  groundFoliage layer it goes to decoBushEU there.
- `ProceduralPlacement` in the i3d is an editor tool only; it does nothing at runtime. `deadwood.i3d` is a standing dead trunk
  (deadwood mission), not a fallen branch – use groundFoliage branches instead.
- Method that worked: plan on a 2 m grid (forest score from large-scale noise, slope, relief and the inside of tight bends so the road
  appears to avoid the forest), keep patches ≥ 2.5 ha and forest edges ≥ 13–19 m from the centreline; fill the interior from validated
  mapEU anchor windows (free of non-natural layers, objects, fields and crops, tree density ≥ 28/ha), 48 m cells with random rotation,
  blended by per-pixel scattering; keep rock/gravel/asphalt in the target (road cuts). Add forest edges (tall weeds, bushes, young
  trees), tree rows on open stretches and boulder groups on slopes.
- Trees with a tall bare trunk look wrong in large numbers. Measured crown start (LOD0Attachments): stonePine_stage03 at 24 m, aspen_stage06_var02 ~8–12 m, beech_stage06_var02 ~10 m; the var01 variants and stage 5 have much lower crowns. Use the tall ones sparingly.
- Bumpy new roads: taking the terrain height under the carriageway from the *nearest* centreline sample (1 m steps) creates irregular steps up to grade × 1 m on steep roads that run diagonally to the height-map grid. Interpolate the height along the arc length instead. Check with the second difference of the DEM along the centreline.
- New rail meshes swept from a template inherit the template's precooked triangle-mesh collision (`tm` attachment), so the collision only exists where the template piece was. Clear the attachment for new or trimmed complex collision shapes (FS25 cooks at runtime, logged as mesh cooking) or add simple convex hulls.
- FS22 maps contain many slightly tilted trees; transfers with rotated frames can push this past 90°. Set every tree node to yaw-only rotation at the end.
- Side roads like the FS25 base game (mapEU `sideRoad_NoSidewalkDecal*`, material `sideRoads_mat`): terrain decal 4 m wide, three vertices across (v 1 / 0.494 / 0, vertex colour B 1 / 0 / 1 for the alpha-noise edge), u along the road 0.0735 per metre, texture `sideRoads_diffuse` (patched asphalt, dirty edges), vertexPaintShader variation `alphaNoise_terrainFormat` with `mBlendedDiffuse/Normal` = `sideRoadTarmac03`, alphaShift 0.25. Recreate the material with the `$data` paths in your own map. A junction without an intersection decal works by starting the side-road decal (decalLayer 1) a little inside the main road's edge strip.
- The asphalt01 terrain layer looks like grey gravel in game. Road verges copied from existing roads carry driveways along; remove asphalt01 outside the decal footprint afterwards (except at junctions).
- Do not force FS22 LOD tree groups (TransformGroup with LOD0/LOD1, e.g. spruceVar) to yaw-only rotation: some use the group rotation to stand the model up and end up lying in the air. Only straighten ReferenceNodes to tree files.
- Track edges in an FS22 GIANTS map: ballast cross-section ±2.77 m; terrain within 3 m is a dirt/grass mix, grass-dirt-patchy from 2.5 m; tall grass (decoFoliage state 10) starts at 2 m and covers ~60–70 % from 2.5–3 m, hiding the ballast edge. Clearing all foliage to the ballast edge leaves a visible green terrain strip.
